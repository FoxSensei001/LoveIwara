Earth and Moon environment

Star positions, visual magnitudes and B-V colour indices are derived from the
HYG Database v4.0 by David Nash / AstroNexus and contributors:
https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database
https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database/blob/main/hyg/CURRENT/LICENSE

Source: hyg/CURRENT/hygdata_v40.csv.gz. This adaptation excludes the Sun, keeps
stars of visual magnitude <= 6.5, rotates the ICRS positions into a fixed viewing
orientation and maps magnitude/colour into displayable star profiles. The
compact source subset is tool/data/quest_starfield/hyg_bright.csv. The sky is
reproducible with tool/generate_quest_starfield.py (numpy and Pillow required).

The rendered panorama adapts the above sources with spherical projection,
lighting, an atmospheric limb and display exposure. Planetary apparent sizes
and positions are composed for viewing comfort, not orbital scale or ephemeris
accuracy. It is a static environment, not the sky for a particular date/place.
The former procedural Milky Way is intentionally absent. All directions,
including the panorama wrap and poles, belong to one complete 360 x 180 sky.
Dynamic mode keeps distant stars in stars.png and shades the Earth and Moon
separately from earth.jpg, clouds.jpg and moon.jpg. These are resized derivatives
of the NASA source images above. The original source maps remain in tool/data.
The offline scene.json provides a fixed composition; globe distances (20/40m)
are artistic VR depth cues, not physical Earth-Moon distances. Surface rotation
and cloud drift are intentionally accelerated, with fixed directional lighting.

The stereo GLES renderer traces the current left/right eye rays into spheres,
then submits them through fixed compositor proxy planes below the media layers.
There is a finite-resolution and view-update tradeoff; this is not native
display-rate sphere geometry. Translated views between updates retain a small
surface-depth reprojection error. Ordinary animation is capped at 30 updates/s;
movement can use up to 60. Actual frame time, compositor cost and power require
Quest hardware measurement. The visible bodies' source textures and mip chains
upload once per renderer lifetime; there are no recurring pixel uploads or readbacks.
Dynamic mode defaults to off. Earth and Moon visibility is independently saved.
The static option selects one baked panorama for the chosen bodies and creates
no orbital renderer. deep_space.png shows both; deep_space_earth.png and
deep_space_moon.png show one body; stars.png shows neither. In dynamic mode,
hidden bodies have no compositor surface or GPU work. Hiding both uses only stars.png.

Rebuild with Python 3.10+, numpy and Pillow:
  python3 tool/generate_quest_starfield.py --runtime-assets android/app/src/quest/assets/environments
Optional perspective and interactive 360-degree inspection:
  python3 tool/generate_quest_starfield.py --preview /tmp/quest-sky/preview.png --preview-dir /tmp/quest-sky
  python3 -m http.server 8768 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory /tmp/quest-sky
Regression checks:
  python3 -m unittest discover -s tool -p test_quest_starfield.py
Dynamic browser preview (same orbital.glsl as the app):
  python3 tool/generate_quest_starfield.py --dynamic-preview-dir /tmp/quest-orbit
  python3 -m http.server 8769 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory /tmp/quest-orbit
